Servo work in my hands. A banana story in Kora's head.

This was not filler while I was bored. I was replacing old servos after a smoking cable incident and testing movement on the plastic legs. At the same time I was testing how Kora handles incomplete prompts, improvisation, personality and mood.

Why the strange story is useful

My request was cut off around “a banana and ba…”. Instead of a polished demo prompt, Kora received an imperfect real-world input. That makes it a better test: does she freeze, become generic, turn into a polite secretary, or continue in a recognisably Kora-like way?

What shapes a Kora story

1 · The prompt

The topic can be precise, absurd or even incomplete. It is the starting point, not a stored script.

2 · Current context

The surrounding conversation and current system state can change what details Kora chooses and how she connects them.

3 · Mood Panel

Mood is not supposed to decide whether the request was understood. It shapes the character of the answer: humour, sarcasm, warmth, curiosity, energy and how restrained or playful the reply becomes.

4 · Local generation

Kora generates the wording rather than replaying a pre-written story. That is why the same idea can take a different route on another run.

Does Kora literally guarantee a never-repeated story?

No hard guarantee is claimed here. The important part is that Kora is not selecting one fixed story from a library. The prompt, context, Mood Panel and generative output can change between runs, so a repeated request can produce a different story. A strict anti-duplicate memory/hash layer would be a separate feature and should only be called ‘guaranteed unique’ after it is implemented and tested.

Hardware test happening at the same time

The aluminium-leg servos are being replaced because an old cable from that set emitted smoke. Until the questionable servos and wiring are replaced and checked, movement tests are being done on the plastic setup first. The story experiment happened during that real workshop work.
