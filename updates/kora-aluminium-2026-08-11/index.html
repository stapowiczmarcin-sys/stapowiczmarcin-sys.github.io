01 / VERIFIED MILESTONE

What actually happened

No pretending that a twitch is already a finished gait. Episode #3 confirms that the new power and servo-control path can physically move the aluminium leg mechanism.

1Battery pack

The pack was assembled and prepared as the energy source for the leg-power system.

2BMS

The battery-management/protection board was soldered into the pack's power path before installation.

3Installed inside Kora

Battery, protection and wiring were physically fitted into the robot body instead of remaining a bench-only prototype.

4Servo-controller test

The 32-channel controller was powered with the leg system connected, producing the first visible twitch/movement.

Important: Kora is not walking yet. The verified result is the first physical response of the metal-leg system. Controlled walking tests are episode #4.
02 / PARTS IN THIS BUILD

What actually went into this test

This was not simply 'connect a battery and hope'. The test joined the battery, protection, power conversion, wiring and the USB servo-control layer into one real system inside Kora.

Power source

Custom battery pack

Assembled specifically for Kora's drive-power stage, then fitted inside the body.

Protection

BMS

Soldered directly into the battery power path.

Motion control

USB 32-channel servo controller

One board provides 32 PWM channels, USB control and a separate servo-power input.

Actuation

18-servo metal-leg system

The controller drives the leg servos as one coordinated mechanical system.

Power stage

DC-DC converter documented on the bench

The build-log hardware includes the converter marked 8–40 V input and 6 V output.

System split

Leg power is separate from the Pi

This episode tests the high-current leg/servo path.

Why this matters: the first twitch validates a complete chain — battery → BMS → power stage → wiring → servo controller → servo channels → metal mechanics.
